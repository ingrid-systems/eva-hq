# Micro-Specificity

## What this is

The core writing principle that makes content convert instead of just getting saves. Micro-specificity means going 3-4 layers deeper than the surface until the reader thinks "How does she know this about me?" This is not a style choice. It's the mechanism that builds trust with premium clients.

**Golden rule:** If your hook would also fit their best friend, go 2 layers deeper.

---

## Why this matters

Value content (tips, steps, how-to's) grows audiences. Micro-specific content converts them. The difference: value content makes people think "that's useful", micro content makes people think "she's watching me."

Premium clients don't buy from free information. They buy from the person who sees their exact situation, names what they're experiencing, and makes them feel seen, heard, and understood. You do that by describing their micro-symptoms, not by explaining causes or teaching solutions.

---

## The 4-layer depth model

Every topic can be written at 4 levels of depth. Layers 0-2 are where most content lives. Layers 3-4 are where conversion happens.

### Layer 0: Generic
The broad topic. Could apply to anyone in any situation.

### Layer 1: Direction
A specific angle within the topic. Still general, still interchangeable.

### Layer 2: Specific problem
A named, recognizable problem. Getting warmer, but still not personal enough.

### Layer 3: Micro-symptom (behavior)
The exact thing they DO. Daily actions, habits, patterns they would recognize as their own. This is where they start reading slower.

### Layer 4: Hidden pain (emotion)
What they FEEL but don't say out loud. The shame, the frustration, the inner dialogue they haven't told anyone. This is where they screenshot your post and send it to a friend with "this is literally me."

---

## Layer-by-layer examples

### Example 1: Business / online entrepreneur

- **Layer 0:** "Grow your business"
- **Layer 1:** "Get more clients"
- **Layer 2:** "You post every day but nobody buys"
- **Layer 3:** "You share your entire process in Stories, give away tips, do Lives, answer every DM with 3-minute voice notes, and all you get back is 'so inspiring!' and zero sign-ups"
- **Layer 4:** "You know your work delivers results. Your clients rave about you. But you feel like the best-kept secret in your niche. And secretly you think: if I just give a little more, it'll come. But it doesn't."

### Example 2: Health / nutrition

- **Layer 0:** "Eat healthier"
- **Layer 1:** "Stop emotional eating"
- **Layer 2:** "You eat an entire bag of chips while watching Netflix"
- **Layer 3:** "At 3pm you grab a cookie with your coffee. At 5pm you 'taste' the kids' dinner. At 9pm the chips are open and you tell yourself: tomorrow I'll start fresh. Every night the same promise."
- **Layer 4:** "You've tried a meal plan three times. Week one was perfect. Week two you had cake at a birthday and thought: it's ruined anyway. You're not ashamed of the eating. You're ashamed that you promised yourself again and broke it again."

### Example 3: Relationships / dating

- **Layer 0:** "Find the right partner"
- **Layer 1:** "Stop attracting the wrong people"
- **Layer 2:** "You keep attracting people who are emotionally unavailable"
- **Layer 3:** "You check your phone every 10 minutes after a good date. You analyze every text with your friends. He replies after 6 hours and you consider waiting 8 so you don't seem desperate."
- **Layer 4:** "You're successful, your life is together, your friends say 'how are you still single?' And you think: maybe I'm just too much. Not too much for someone else. Too much for yourself to believe someone can handle it."

## The digging process: 5 questions

Before writing any hook or content piece, answer these questions. They force you from surface to micro.

### Question 1: Who exactly is this for?
Not who your work *could* help. The specific person you'd love to work with. What does their day look like? What's their revenue, their situation, their stage?

### Question 2: Where are they stuck right now?
Specific, with numbers and context. Not "they want more clients" but "they're stuck at 8K months, doing everything manually, posting 5x a week with zero DMs."

### Question 3: How is this showing up for them daily?
This is where you go micro. What do they DO? What does their Tuesday afternoon look like? What do they catch themselves doing at 10pm? Describe the behavior so precisely they think you're describing their life.

### Question 4: What do they think is the problem vs. what is it actually?
They think it's visibility. You know it's messaging. They think they need more followers. You know they need to stop talking to everyone and start talking to one person. This gap is your million dollar insight.

### Question 5: What have they already tried?
This proves you understand their journey, not just their current state. "You've done the masterclass. You've downloaded the freebie. You've tried posting Reels every day for 30 days. None of it moved the needle." When you name what they've tried, they trust you to name what will actually work.

## Symptoms vs. causes in micro-specificity

This principle is critical: always lead with SYMPTOMS (what they experience), not CAUSES (why it happens).

**Cause-focused (don't lead with this):**
"You have a messaging problem."

**Symptom-focused (lead with this):**
"You post every day. You share tips, do Lives, answer every DM. Your audience says 'so inspiring!' And then they buy from someone else who posted half as much as you did."

The cause comes later, in your reframe or solution. First make them feel seen by describing what they live through. Then educate them on WHY.

## Constriction + expansion: writing for premium clients

Micro-specific content for premium clients speaks to both sides simultaneously:

**Constriction** (where they feel stuck):
"It's frustrating seeing people with half your talent make ten times what you're making."

**Expansion** (what they know is possible):
"You know if someone just saw your work, they'd stay forever. Because your clients get insane results."

The combination is key. You're not speaking to someone in lack ("you have no clients, you don't know what you're doing"). You're speaking to someone who holds the tension between where they are and where they know they could be.

**Lack language (avoid):** "You're barely showing up. Nobody comments. You're ready to quit."
**Million dollar client language (use):** "You're doing everything right on paper. And it's still not converting. Not because your work isn't good enough. Because your content is putting you in the friend zone instead of the getting-paid zone."

## The micro-specificity test

For every hook and every piece of content, run these checks:

1. **Best friend test:** Would this also describe their best friend's situation? If yes, go deeper.
2. **Watching test:** Would they think "she's literally watching my life"? If not, add specific behaviors.
3. **Shame test:** Does it touch something they haven't said out loud? That's where the real connection lives.
4. **Screenshot test:** Would they screenshot this and send it to someone with "this is me"? That's layer 3-4.
5. **Internal voice test:** Does it sound like their inner dialogue at 11pm? Not their Instagram caption voice, their real voice.

## Common mistakes

### Going wide instead of deep
"You struggle with time management, client acquisition, and self-doubt" names three problems at layer 1. Pick ONE and go to layer 4.

### Describing the cause instead of the symptom
"You have limiting beliefs around money" is a cause. "You calculate your hourly rate before every proposal and then round it down 'just to be safe'" is a symptom.

### Using coach language instead of client language
"You're not aligned with your pricing" is coach-speak. "You know your package is worth 5K but you keep saying 2,500 because you're afraid they'll ghost you" is client language at layer 3-4.

### Staying at layer 2 and thinking it's specific enough
"You post every day but nobody buys" feels specific. But it's layer 2. It's still true for thousands of people. The micro version describes WHAT they post, HOW they feel after posting, and WHAT they tell themselves when nothing happens.

### Teaching the solution instead of creating the aha
Micro-specificity is about making them problem-aware and solution-aware, not about teaching them the steps. You share value through analogies, stories, and examples that anchor your point. Focus on the "aha" moment, not the "here are the 5 steps."

## Sentence forms that read as AI

Separate from depth: some sentence *shapes* now read as AI-generated regardless of how specific the content is. They are a surface tell. Avoid them as a default. The underlying mechanism is fine, the canned shape is not.

### The antithesis with parallelism: "not X, it's Y"
Most flagged form. In Dutch: "Dit is geen X-probleem, dit is een Y-probleem." / "Het is niet X. Het is Y." In English: "It's not about X. It's about Y." / "It's not just X, it's Y."

This is the default surface form of the **Reframe** mechanism, so the temptation is constant. Keep the reframe, drop the shape. Deliver the redirect through:
- **a scene:** show the real problem in concrete behavior, never naming "not X but Y"
- **a story:** "My client thought her problem was visibility. She had three weeks of unread DMs."
- **a question:** "What if it was never about your prices?"
- **a plain statement of the real cause** without negating the assumed one in parallel

### Adjacent tells from the same family
Pre-empt these too, they belong to the same rhythm:
- The triadic rhetorical list: "X, Y, and Z" as a flourish ("not louder, not faster, just clearer")
- "Let that sink in." / "And that changes everything." / "Here's the thing."
- "The truth is..." / "The real question is..." as openers

### This is a living list
AI tells shift over time. When a form starts reading as machine-written, add it here. The rule is constant: keep the mechanism, change the shape, and let the human's own rewrite carry the final voice.
